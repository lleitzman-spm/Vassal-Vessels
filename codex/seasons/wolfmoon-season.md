Men do not march in winter. Everyone knows it, which is precisely why marching in winter works: the enemy is not looking. You pay for it in every other column — twenty points off every house's willingness, marching at little over half speed, tiredness climbing half again as fast, almost nothing to forage, and one man in a hundred lost every day past the fifth. In exchange the battle starts with up to half a point of surprise, and surprise is worth more than numbers.
